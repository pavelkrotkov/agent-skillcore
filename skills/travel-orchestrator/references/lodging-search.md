# Lodging search

## Procedure

1. Compare neighborhoods first: commute/airport access, transit, safety context, noise, family practicality, groceries, and the activities that drive the trip. Then search properties in the best two or three neighborhoods.
2. Search the real dates and occupancy, including children ages, room count, bed/bath needs, kitchen/laundry, accessibility, parking, board/meals, and cancellation. If the user requests or prefers a platform, search it first; record every requested source as searched, blocked/unavailable, or `not_searched` using [provenance.md](provenance.md), and retain useful cross-listed alternatives. A platform preference is not an exclusivity rule unless the user says so. Compare hotels, serviced apartments/whole homes, and all-inclusives only on equivalent dates, party, and room needs.
3. Treat list prices as discovery only. With `trvl`, use `search_accommodations` first; use `search_hotels_with_details`, `hotel_rooms`, or direct provider detail for finalists. With Toolkit, use `compare-hotels` and relevant portal/metasearch/apartment skills for discovery, then apply this file's exact-unit rules before ranking.
4. For every finalist, extend the decisive observation record with the exact property and room/unit, seller, dates, occupancy, bedrooms versus beds, bathrooms, kitchen, pool access and operating/seasonal status, actual beach access/walk/route, parking access/cost, booked board basis, and source/booking link. Keep unknown fields unknown. A resort name, star/luxury label, "beachfront" category, or all-inclusive property category does not prove meals, pool operation, or direct beach access.
5. Verify the seller's itemized dated quote. Expense includes the displayed/base stay plus mandatory taxes and mandatory cleaning, service, resort/destination, local, protection, or other required charges. "All fees included" does not establish that taxes are included. If the seller requires a choice between a nonrefundable protection fee and a refundable damage deposit/authorization, show each choice separately: the protection fee is expense; the refundable deposit is separate cash exposure, not expense or a discount. Do not combine one seller's price with another seller's terms.
6. Record the cancellation/no-show cutoff as the exact property-local date, time, and timezone when available, plus payment timing. A date without its local cutoff/timezone remains unresolved at a boundary; do not silently reinterpret it in the traveler's timezone.
7. Summarize reviews from observed sources with retrieval date/recency. Separate recurring family-relevant patterns (space, food/board, beach/pool, noise, room condition, service) from isolated anecdotes, listing claims, and inference. An explicit missing safety requirement or other material safety concern places the candidate on hold until resolved; do not recommend an unsafe lead because it is cheaper.
8. Rank verified fit and complete expense, not platform brand. Give booking links, concise pros/cons, and near-tie finalists. Do not generalize that one platform has globally better quality from a few listings.

## Interpretation

Airbnb documents fee-inclusive display before taxes in the US, but taxes a host or jurisdiction collects separately may still be absent; the FTC's US short-term-lodging rule requires upfront total-price disclosure but does not remove the need to inspect the provider breakdown. Booking.com's terms distinguish property-supplied taxes, surcharges, availability, and cancellation policies. Toolkit `compare-hotels` sample totals and benefit-adjusted values are discovery guidance, not substitutes for exact seller expense or board/amenity evidence. Use the provider's own final breakdown as the stronger evidence.

# Airfare search

## Procedure

1. Start with Toolkit `plan-trip` or `compare-flights`; load `flight-search-strategy` and `lessons-learned` for complex or award searches. Use the bounded airport/carrier coverage in [airport-coverage.md](airport-coverage.md): search the requested pair first, then relevant accepted/nearby gateways and any materially different carrier coverage. Record each airport pair rather than hiding it under a metro label.
2. For date flexibility, search the Cartesian product of departure and return dates inside the allowed window, plus plausible stay lengths. A calendar/date-grid result is a lead; retrieve a concrete itinerary before ranking it. Never infer a best booking weekday from folklore.
3. Before ranking a flight quote, record its ticket construction (`round_trip`, `separate_one_ways`, or mixed/self-transfer) and preserve each direction/segment separately: travel date; origin/destination; local departure/arrival time, local calendar day, and timezone/offset (or explicit unknown); marketing/operating carrier and flight number; fare family; personal-item/carry-on allowance; checked-bag terms; seat rules; and change/cancellation rules. A cabin filter, “Economy” result, or one-leg “Main” label cannot classify the whole itinerary.
4. Keep a quote's price and terms on the same seller/product evidence. Verify that a round-trip total covers both directions; do not promote an outbound teaser to a trip total. Label two independent one-ways, mixed carriers, mixed fare families, separate tickets, airport changes, and self-transfers explicitly, including baggage recheck and missed-connection responsibility. Enforce any same-airport constraint on the actual return airport.
5. Price the requested party, then requested baggage/seat add-ons. Multiply per-traveler fares or fees by travelers, but multiply a shared checked bag by the number of bags charged on each direction, not by passenger count. Mark policy-derived bag amounts as estimates and seller-priced add-ons as priced. An adult-only benchmark stays labeled as a benchmark until age-specific traveler pricing/occupancy is available; discovery queries use generic counts/ages only, never passenger names or minor identifiers.
6. Compare Basic against standard economy only after included personal-item/overhead carry-on, seat, change/cancel, and bag benefits are normalized. A cheaper Basic headline cannot outrank an option whose required benefits are included until missing add-on prices are known or clearly estimated.
7. Use `trvl` selectively for date windows, nearby-airport expansion, split-ticket/positioning, and an independent check of finalists. If structured sources omit a materially plausible carrier or small-airport route, follow the direct carrier gap check in [airport-coverage.md](airport-coverage.md). Use structured Toolkit sources before browser automation; browser-verify only a finalist or that material gap.

## Interpretation

Toolkit examples that use the cheapest bookable fare class for cpp are candidate-generation guidance, not a ranking rule; local leg/product and whole-party normalization controls recommendations. Google says its “best” ranking trades off price and convenience and exposes date, price-graph, airport, and bag-fee views; use those as search dimensions, not as a guarantee. `trvl` supports explicit date windows, checked-bag filters, nearby-airport/deep expansion, and all-in pricing when a profile contains status. Toolkit source-priority and fallback examples are candidate-generation guidance; this skill assigns confidence from query-matched inspected evidence and actual harness capabilities. Results can be stale, married-segment-dependent, currency-mismatched, or provider-specific.
